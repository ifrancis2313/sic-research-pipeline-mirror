# Block 3 splits into exposure optimization + human stock-selection loop, not a single-shot stock-level optimizer

QEPM's Block 3 does not directly output stock-level positions. It outputs **exposure targets** (a dollar amount against some combination of sector/factor/screen dimensions, buy- or sell-direction) which are then packaged into **research briefs** and handed to analysts — of mixed quant skill, many bound for IB/fundamental roles rather than quant seats — who research and pitch specific stocks (DCF, screens) to fill or trim that exposure. The fundamental rubric determines whether a competing pitch is ready or needs revision; it does not statistically select a winner. The system supplies portfolio-fit information and a binding risk check, while students argue the fundamental case and the executive board decides whether to invest. The engine recommends a position size from the actual portfolio context rather than choosing a winner: 3% is the normal initial size, it may recommend 1–5%, and movement above 5% requires a new student reassessment, an engine recommendation, a fresh binding risk check, and PM approval; the hard single-name cap remains 10%.

This deliberately breaks a strict one-way composition (`f(g(h(z(x))))`) — there's a human research step and feedback loop wedged between exposure-target generation and final position sizing. The alternative (a single-shot optimizer emitting stock-level weights directly) was rejected because it would leave no role for less-quantitative analysts and would forgo the program's training goal, not because it was infeasible.
