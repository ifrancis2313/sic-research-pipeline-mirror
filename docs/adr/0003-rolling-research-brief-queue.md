# Research briefs run as a rolling weekly queue with an explicit research window

The engine may propose a ranked queue from factor gaps and portfolio constraints, but the PM publishes the final agenda: they may reorder, suppress, or add a brief before students see it. The PM publishes that fresh portfolio pulse and a ranked queue of up to five research briefs after each Friday market close, then reviews it at the following club meeting. Fewer than five briefs is valid when the portfolio has fewer genuine needs; the process must not invent work to fill a quota. Each brief declares its priority and pod capacity, and more than one pod may intentionally work the same high-priority brief to create competing pitches. A pressing need may instead be a **Research campaign**: a linked package of buy and/or sell briefs. The campaign is not all-or-nothing: individual pitches/trade legs may be rejected or approved at a smaller size, but the final risk check must then evaluate the actual approved subset rather than treating its trades as independent. A brief that a pod has claimed remains visible as **in progress** even if its original portfolio priority falls; only untouched briefs may be suspended immediately. Each active brief records its owner, claimed date, target pitch date, and latest progress update. It receives a progress check after two weeks and must be pitched, explicitly extended, narrowed, or released by weeks three to four; two meetings without a meaningful update releases it automatically. This keeps enough work available for all pods without allowing unowned or stalled work to occupy the queue indefinitely, while a fresh pre-trade risk check remains required before any trade.

## Initial published-brief standard

Every published brief includes its portfolio need and direction; factor objective and avoidances; why it matters now; pod capacity and research deadline; applicable constraints (including liquidity, position room, and existing overlap); and required pitch outputs: thesis, DCF, CCA, catalysts, and risks. This is an initial working template that the PM may add to or simplify as the club learns; it is meant to frame independent research, not prescribe a ticker or an investment conclusion.

## Candidate precheck

Students may submit prospective tickers throughout the week. The PM targets an advisory fit-card response within two business days. The card reports eligibility, factor and beta effect, liquidity, position/sleeve room, and crowding concerns, but neither rejects the research nor reserves portfolio capacity. The binding risk check reruns after a pitch has been approved for a proposed trade.

## Liquidity control

The binding pre-trade check requires at least $5 million median daily dollar volume over the preceding 30 trading days. A proposed net buy or sell may not exceed 2% of that median daily dollar volume. The rule applies to stocks and ETFs. An exception follows the portfolio exception process.

## Alternatives considered

A strict one-brief-at-a-time FIFO queue (publish exactly one need, hold the next until it resolves) was rejected: with a two-to-four-week research window per brief, it would starve most pods of work most weeks and directly undermines the training-program goal of keeping every pod usefully occupied, not just one at a time. Publishing every detected factor gap unfiltered, with no PM curation step, was also rejected: the club cannot staff an unbounded number of simultaneous briefs, and removing PM judgment from what gets published is exactly the "invent work to fill a quota" failure mode this ADR's own opening paragraph explicitly rules out. The five-item rolling cap with PM override is the point in between that keeps genuine work flowing without manufacturing hollow assignments.
