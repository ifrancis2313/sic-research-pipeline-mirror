# Use the Russell 1000 as the stable benchmark and fund approved ideas from excess broad-core ETFs

SIC will measure portfolio beta and performance against the Russell 1000, using IWB as the daily implementation proxy. The benchmark remains stable as the portfolio transitions and is not the inherited IWB/SPY holdings mix. SIC targets a portfolio beta of 0.75–0.80, begins a warning band at 0.90, and applies a hard ceiling at 1.00. A single anomalous 90-day reading does not force action; a sustained breach or agreement with the 252-day context invokes the exception process. If inherited holdings already exceed the ceiling, SIC does not force an immediate sale, but a new trade cannot worsen the breach without an approved exception. The long-run core market sleeve is 20–25%; IWB is its intended broad-market implementation and SPY is transition inventory to reduce through Faculty Advisor's advisor review/execution process. SIC has no strategic cash sleeve: 0–5% is normal operating cash, and cash above 5% is flagged as a funding opportunity rather than automatically invested. The board may deliberately carry a higher cash balance, which the system records and surfaces without forcing deployment. When an approved active or experimental investment needs funding, the portfolio produces a funding recommendation: use available cash/dividends first, then reduce broad-core ETF exposure above its target band. It must not automatically sell active stocks, factor-tilt holdings, or experimental holdings merely to finance a new pitch.

## Alternatives considered

Immediately liquidating the inherited IWB/SPY position to fund the active and experimental sleeves in one step was rejected: it would force an uncontrolled, all-at-once realized-gain/loss event and abandon the 20–25% core sleeve's stabilizing role in a single trade, undermining the benchmark stability this ADR exists to establish. Leaving ETF-funded transitions ad hoc — the PM deciding case by case whether to sell core exposure to fund a new pitch, with no standing rule — was also rejected: every other binding control in this pipeline (liquidity, beta, concentration) is a fail-closed, auditable rule rather than a discretionary call, and a special-cased "PM just decides" funding path would be the one place capital actually moves without that same discipline. The chosen order (cash and dividends first, then excess broad-core ETF exposure above its target band, never active or experimental holdings) keeps the funding mechanism itself governed by an explicit, checkable rule rather than a judgment call made fresh each time.
